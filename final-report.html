Rook Dispatch · PM report · 16 October 2026

Release 4.2 didn't have a quiet August.
It had a bug that taught the system to stop asking people.

What happened, the evidence from six working sessions, the fix we'd build, and the habit that stops the next one.

Prepared for Helen Achebe, Director of Product. Fictional teaching scenario: Rook Industries is not a real company.

What happened, plainly

On 12 August we shipped two changes at once. Responders started missing pings, and four of them stopped getting any.

1. The clock got shorter

A ping used to wait 90 seconds before counting as missed. 4.2 cut it to 60, for everyone. Slow answers became misses.

2. A miss costs as much as a "no"

The score takes −0.12 for a miss, the same as a turn-down. It adds +0.08 only when someone takes a callout. No decay, no reset.

3. No way back

Low score means rarely asked. Someone who is never pinged can't earn points back. Four responders slid out and stayed out.

X-Ray Vision · the code

Finding: the score only goes up when someone takes a callout, so a quiet responder can never climb back.

Starts at 0.5
Taken: +0.08
Missed: −0.12
Turned down: −0.12
Gain for a take+0.08
Cost of anything not taken−0.12

One miss wipes out one and a half takes. No time window, no decay, no reset, no override. No pings means no takes, so no way up. The code throws away the difference between "slow" and "no" on purpose.

Putting it together

The likeliest story: a loop with no exit.

1 · Shorter waitPing wait 90s → 60s
→
2 · More missesSlow answers expire
→
3 · Score drops−0.12 per miss
→
4 · Fewer pingsLow rank, rarely asked
→
5 · Can't recoverNo pings, no +0.08
↺ and round again. Nothing in the system breaks the loop, and handlers read quiet weeks as normal.
STRONG

Misses jumped after 4.2 for all 16 responders. Four lost most of their pings. Not weather, geography, volume or time of day.

LIKELY

Misses lower rank and fewer pings follow. For the four, misses spike the week 4.2 ships and pings collapse 1–2 weeks later.

UNPROVEN

The rank effect itself. The database has no ranking scores, time-to-accept or push logs. Still needed: Wen Li, the real 4.2 diff, pings after 6 Sep.

The fix we'd build (a proposal, not agreed)

Make going quiet visible, and give people a way back.

Now: restore 90s

Separate and immediate. Explains the "gone before I could answer" tickets. Needs Marcus and Helen first.

1. "Gone quiet" alert

Handlers see it when a responder's pings drop under half of their own 7-day baseline. Closes the 3–5 day lag before tickets.

2. Guaranteed re-entry ping

A quiet responder gets at least one matching ping every 48 hours, and their phone says why.

3. Lighter penalty for a miss

−0.06 for a miss vs −0.12 for a turn-down, to be tuned with Wen.

Availability Confidence is deliberately out of scope. Penalty values and the 48-hour rule are untested.

Keeping it from happening again

Each check in review-checklist is aimed at something that went wrong in 4.2.

1
Owner named
Every Q3 roadmap row still lists Priya, who left 21 Aug. Marcus's 14 Aug question about who the ranking change applied to sat unanswered because nobody clearly owned it.
2
Success measure
"It's a seasonal dip" was said by the handoff and by Marcus, and nobody had shown numbers. A stated measure forces the question "what would we see if this worked?"
3
Scope matches start to end
Availability Confidence was committed for 4.2 and dropped with no decision. The ranking weights and the ping wait shipped together, so the cause was confounded.
4
Problem before the fix
The ranking change has no doc and its intent was never written down. A brief that states the problem first makes the intent reviewable before it ships.
5
Dates received and due
The roadmap was last reviewed 30 Jun and was stale by October. Dates make a stale commitment visible.

These are our reading of where each check would have raised a flag, not a record that it would have prevented 4.2.
